About Me

March 9, 2026 · 2 min · Sabrina Adler

Hello! I’m Sabrina Adler, an Integrated B.S./M.S. student in Computer Science and Engineering at The Pennsylvania State University, where I am also completing a B.S. in Mathematics (Systems Analysis) and I am in the Schreyer Honors College.

My interests sit at the intersection of machine learning, computer vision, and scientific research, particularly where computational methods can help us better understand complex natural systems.

Research#

I am currently conducting master’s thesis research under Prof. Mehrdad Mahdavi (Computer Science) and Prof. Christina Grozinger (Ecology).

My thesis, PolliSense, develops a scalable computer vision framework for assessing pollinator habitat quality from ground-level imagery. The project integrates segmentation, representation learning, ecological feature extraction, and preference-based learning to estimate interpretable habitat quality scores from large collections of images.

The goal is to connect modern machine learning systems with ecological field knowledge, enabling large-scale monitoring of pollinator habitats using computer vision.

This research is supported by the NSF INSECT NET graduate training program.

Industry Experience#

I previously worked as a Data Automation and Machine Learning Intern at Merck & Co., where I designed machine learning systems for pharmaceutical manufacturing environments.

My work included developing anomaly detection systems for temperature-controlled units, building scalable AWS machine learning infrastructure, and deploying production inference systems used by engineering teams.

Additionally, I worked as a Research and Development Engineer Intern at Penn State’s Applied Research Laboratory, where I developed LLM infrastructure and retrieval-augmented generation systems for large-scale model serving.

Teaching and Outreach#

I enjoy teaching and communicating technical ideas.

I have contributed instructional material for the graduate course Technology and AI in Living Systems, where students built complete object detection pipelines and deployed machine learning models through APIs.

I also frequently give technical talks through the Penn State Math Club, covering topics ranging from neural networks to algorithms.

Leadership#

I currently serve as President of the Penn State Math Club, where I organize lectures, competitions, and student-faculty events. I also founded Penn State’s Directed Reading Program (DRP), which pairs undergraduate students with graduate mentors for semester-long guided readings in advanced mathematics.

Interests#

My academic interests include:

  • Machine learning
  • Computer vision
  • representation learning
  • scientific computing
  • applied mathematics
  • ecological data science

Outside of research and coursework, I enjoy teaching mathematics, birding, and cooking.
